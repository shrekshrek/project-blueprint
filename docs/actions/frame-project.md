# frame-project

Establish whether the project is worth pursuing and what decision the blueprint must enable.

## Use when

Value, desired outcome, sponsor, constraints, feasibility, evidence, or success criteria are unclear or disputed. It may be skipped when an accepted charter covers the same scope and has not materially changed.

## Inputs

Project idea or charter, stakeholder statements, business evidence, existing systems, constraints, deadlines, economics, compliance context, and known unknowns.

## Method

1. State the problem without embedding a preferred solution.
2. Identify desired outcomes, users/stakeholders, sponsor, success signals, non-goals, and decision deadline.
3. Separate evidence, inference, assumption, conflict, and unknown.
4. Assess feasibility across product/adoption, operations/process, technology/data, and delivery/economic/compliance dimensions.
5. Rank unknowns by impact and uncertainty; use `evidence-researcher` for volatile, external, or conflicting facts.
6. Propose go, conditional-go, research-first, narrow, or stop, with rationale.
7. For Standard/Enterprise work—or weak value evidence—invoke `product-challenger` before the gate.
8. Ask only questions that can change viability, scope direction, or success.

## Outputs

Charter, feasibility decision, success measures, constraints, glossary seed, non-goals, research/validation questions, and unresolved blockers.

## Completion

The problem is worth solving or explicitly researchable, the decision context is clear, and no hidden blocker makes deeper planning misleading.

## Avoid

Feature brainstorming before value is established, fabricated market evidence, treating implementation as proof of demand, and continuing past a known non-viable constraint.
